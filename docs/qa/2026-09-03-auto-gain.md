# Auto Gain implementation and verification

Auto Gain prepares microphone levels with a small, local calibration routine. It does not continuously ride the singer's volume. No audio recordings, services, new dependencies, or saved calibration profiles are involved.

## User behavior

- Simple inserts Auto Gain before the effects when starting or loading a sound. Existing manual settings are retained when carrying the utility into a new sound.
- A saved chain already using all 16 slots starts unchanged. Simple explains that Auto Gain needs a free slot. Loading another full preset while retaining Auto Gain refuses without replacing the current sound.
- Advanced exposes Auto Gain as a normal effect. Drag it, use Alt+Left/Right to reorder it, bypass it, remove it, or add it from the Effects rack. Its position, mode, manual adjustment, and bypass state survive preset loads unless the loaded preset explicitly contains its own Auto Gain settings. A shorter preset keeps it before the terminal limiter.
- Auto mode asks for a few phrases and pauses. It waits for enough level variation, learns one bounded adjustment, then holds it. Recheck starts a new measurement; device and channel changes reset automatic learning. Manual mode uses the gain control.
- An interface carrying the microphone on only one channel selects that channel. Identical dual mono selects one copy without averaging. Silence retains the selection. Different active sources, including another source starting after the singer pauses, require an explicit channel choice. Selecting Automatic channel again restarts that choice.
- The MIC IN meter continues to show raw capture. Its numbers are dBFS, relative to digital full scale, not a measurement of acoustic sound pressure or microphone quality. A quiet microphone can legitimately read lower than a studio setup.
- Waveform duplication on Advanced is outside this change. The pre-existing waveform display edits remain separate.

## Audio contract

The graph is raw capture -> channel selection -> ordered effects -> existing protected output path. Auto Gain can occupy any normal effect position in Advanced. Raw capture also feeds MIC IN and emergency Bypass; neither includes the learned boost.

Auto mode requires at least three seconds of observations with useful phrases and pauses. A rolling window estimates the noise floor, useful level, and observed peak. The target is -24 dBFS RMS, subject to a -12 dBFS observed-peak allowance and a gain range of -24 to +24 dB. Adjustments ramp over 100 ms. Silence and steady noise do not qualify for increasing gain.

The Auto Gain worklet separately bounds every output sample at -12 dBFS with shared stereo peak reduction and 250 ms recovery. That bound runs on the audio thread, including in Manual mode and while the main thread stalls. It is a sample-peak bound, not a true-peak or acoustic loudness guarantee. The existing final attenuation, limiter, emergency Bypass, and watchdog still apply. Auto Gain is limited to one instance per chain and is excluded from the existing +12 dB effect gain estimate because its own output is bounded.

An active Auto Gain in Auto mode adapts downstream gate thresholds to the measured noise/voice gap. The preset threshold remains the stored baseline; Advanced shows an effective threshold when it differs. Moving Auto Gain after a gate, bypassing it, removing it, or choosing Manual restores that gate's baseline. Recheck after changing Auto Gain's position or processing ahead of it.

Input and output meters measure channels separately. Peak is the maximum channel peak; RMS is the maximum channel RMS. Clip runs are evaluated per channel. The gate and hidden-tab watchdog also inspect every channel. Opposite polarity cannot cancel the detector, and right-only input is not diluted by a silent left channel.

The existing `get_capabilities` policy response includes the current view, input/channel diagnostics, and the Auto Gain adjustment and state. Channel indices are zero-based. Agents can edit the ordinary utility parameters and chain position; channel choice and Recheck remain explicitly human-operated controls. `get_chain` keeps its preset-shaped round-trip contract.

## Verification

Automated coverage lives in:

- `tests/test-input-calibration.js`: silence, steady noise, phrases, fixed gain, clipping, gate bounds, simultaneous and sequential sources, exact silent gaps in dual mono, explicit channel choice, and malformed summaries.
- `tests/test-input-worklet.js`: routing, gain ramps, bounded peaks, stereo polarity, finite output, and raw summary levels.
- `tests/test-auto-gain-session.js`: effect recreation, preset retention, bypass, manual mode, gate placement, device reset, clipping recheck, disposal, stale start/device preparation completions, pending module loads, and module/node-construction failures with unadjusted fallback.
- `tests/test-channel-analysis.js`: right-only -20 dBFS sine peak and -23.0103 dBFS RMS, opposite polarity, independent clip runs, and more than two interface channels.
- Existing gate, hidden-tab watchdog, lifecycle, preset, policy, Undo, and graph suites. The preset tool suite also covers Simple insertion through agent loads, retained Advanced settings, manual gain policy, duplicate refusal, and restoration of a full saved chain.

Browser verification used a separate localhost:8001 test origin, synthetic MediaStreams, and a muted destination. No physical microphone recording or speaker listening was used. Observed results:

- Quiet synthetic phrases learned positive fixed gain, including +19 dB with exact zero-valued pauses. Repeated pauses kept the same channel and did not produce an ambiguity warning.
- Preset loads retained learned gain. Advanced reordering, Manual mode, preset retention, bypass, removal, and adding the utility used the visible controls.
- Two different synthetic input channels produced a choice prompt; selecting Input 2 selected channel index 1.
- Native OfflineAudioContext rendering of loud opposite-polarity stereo produced a maximum magnitude of 0.2511886358 on both channels, approximately -12 dBFS, and retained opposite polarity.
- No browser console warnings or errors appeared in the final synthetic session.

Run `node tests/run.js`, `npm run build`, and `git diff --check` for the automated/build checks. The repository has no separate lint or typecheck command.

Final automated result on 2026-09-03: a clean copy of base `6527c16` plus only the staged Auto Gain changes passed all 51 test files and 5,537 checks. The static build passed with 61 public files. The mixed working copy also passed, including the separate waveform/dB test work. The clean-copy result is the verification for this change's commit scope. Independent code review completed with no remaining actionable findings.

## Submission acceptance still needed

With headphones, test the MacBook microphone and the studio/interface microphone in the submission browser. Speak or sing normally, pause, and confirm a held adjustment. Then sing quietly and loudly, change presets, and test a device switch. Confirm the gate preserves quiet words, the processed signal is useful, and Stop/Bypass behave as expected. Also run the existing `docs/ACCEPTANCE.md` output/watchdog checks on the actual hardware.

This first version distinguishes phrases from steady levels; it does not separate speech from music. Pause backing music during setup. If the signal is too weak, clipped before capture, or inseparable from room noise, it asks for correction or waits rather than promising to repair it. Safari, physical interfaces, and PA feedback behavior require their own listening checks.

## Post-deploy monitoring and validation

No deployment is included in this work. The submission owner should check the two microphone setups above before submission and after the first deployed load. Healthy behavior is a visible raw input level, a held adjustment after clear phrases, stable channel selection through pauses, and no unexpected output mute. Look in the browser console for `Input channel preparation unavailable`, `Auto Gain unavailable`, worklet errors, or watchdog errors. There is no new telemetry or dashboard.

If a device loses a phrase, calibration changes during a quiet pause, or output rises unexpectedly, stop that session and use the last accepted build until reproduced. Auto Gain can be bypassed or removed in Advanced while diagnosing. Existing safety controls remain available.
