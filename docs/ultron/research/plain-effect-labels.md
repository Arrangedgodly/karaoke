# Research: Plain-Effect Labels — What Words Non-Engineers See For Each Effect

Ticket: [#44](https://github.com/Arrangedgodly/voxchain/issues/44). All primary sources fetched and verified 2026-09-01 unless explicitly flagged. Verbatim quotes are in *italics* and link inline to the source. Where a page could not be fetched (JS-only bodies, 403s) the source is named and the limitation stated; search-snippet-level evidence is always marked **[snippet-verified]** and never used alone for a CANDIDATE.

## Question

For the Simple view, every effect needs a one-line summary of the form **plain language first, technical name quietly after** (e.g. "Evens out loudness · Compressor"). What plain words do consumer vocal apps, karaoke machines, podcast tools, and voice changers actually put in front of compression, reverb, gate, EQ, limiter, pitch shift, autotune, distortion, bitcrusher, tremolo, phaser, delay, and chorus? Deliverable: one CANDIDATE plain label per registered node type, each grounded in surveyed sources, plus a note wherever consumer products disagree.

Two format precedents worth naming up front, because they validate the Simple-view pattern itself:

- **Voloco's help center** ships exactly the paired form, in mirrored order — *"Compressor (Add Punch)"*, *"EQ (Shape Your Tone)"*, *"Echo (Add Delay)"*, *"Reverb (Add Space)"* ([Complete Voloco Effects Guide](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36347496022423-Complete-Voloco-Effects-Guide)). voxchain's Simple view is the same idea with plain-first ordering.
- **GarageBand's iPad Audio Recorder** already runs a two-audience split like Simple/Engineer: *"Tap the Fun or Studio button"*, where *"Fun view lets you quickly change the sound of your recording"* and Studio view *"gives you more options to enhance your recording"* ([Apple support guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-ipad/record-sounds-with-the-audio-recorder-chs39283a21/ipados)).

## Survey — verbatim wording by product

### Voloco (mobile vocal FX app — closest living relative to voxchain)

All quotes from Voloco's official help center, fetched 2026-09-01.

- **Compressor** — [How to Use the Compressor](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36347754757271-How-to-Use-the-Compressor): *"Compression makes your quiet parts louder and your loud parts quieter. This evens out your vocal performance so every word is heard clearly."* The [Complete Effects Guide](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36347496022423-Complete-Voloco-Effects-Guide) pairs it as *"Compressor (Add Punch) — Make your vocals more consistent and punchy."*
- **EQ** — [How to Use EQ](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36347966544791-How-to-Use-EQ): *"EQ (Equalizer) works by lowering or amplifying certain frequencies of a sound, whether high, low, or mid-range, to create a color or effect."* The guide pairs it as *"EQ (Shape Your Tone) — Make your voice brighter, warmer, or cut out problem frequencies."* Free EQ presets include **De-Ess** (the only consumer-visible de-esser name found anywhere in this survey).
- **Delay / Echo** — [How to Use Echo Effects](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36348094184215-How-to-Use-Echo-Effects): *"Echo (also called delay) repeats your vocal after a set amount of time."* Also: *"Echo adds delay to your audio to evoke space or add a rhythmic effect."* The plain/technical pairing: *"Echo (Add Delay) — Create echo effects from subtle to rhythmic repeats."*
- **Reverb** — [How to Add Reverb to Your Vocals](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36348152679191-How-to-Add-Reverb-to-Your-Vocals): *"Reverb creates the sound of a space for the vocals to sit in, whether large or small."* Presets are pure room-scale words: *"Small Club, Medium Room, Large Hall, Cathedral."* The pairing: *"Reverb (Add Space) — Make your vocals sound like they're in a room, hall, or cathedral."*
- **Echo vs Reverb, in Voloco's own words**: *"Echo creates distinct, rhythmic repeats. Reverb creates a wash of reflections that simulate a space."*
- **Gate/noise** — the `Clean` effect: *"uses audio filtering technology to remove background noise while leaving your natural voice intact"* ([Complete Guide](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36347496022423-Complete-Voloco-Effects-Guide)).
- **Autotune** — two speeds: *"Hard Tune — The classic, 'digital' pitch correction used by countless rappers and singers alike"* vs *"Natural Tune"*, which aims at *"a more natural, rounded tone."*
- **Chorus** — *"Big Chorus"*: *"You want big, wide vocals without recording multiple takes."*
- **Bitcrusher** — *"8 Bit Chip — Funky bleeps and bloops inspired by classic video games."* Also *"Phasers on Stun: Bit-crushed rhythmic octave jumps."*
- **Distortion** — *"Light Distortion: Adds mild distortion to the voice"*; *"Cranked"* = *"crunchy, blown-out distortion."*

### Apple: GarageBand + Voice Memos (the consumer-glossary goldmine)

- **[GarageBand iPad effect plug-ins guide](https://support.apple.com/en-is/guide/garageband-ipad/chs39d470b96/ipados)** — Apple's own plain-language glossary:
  - Compressor: *"A compressor adjusts the volume of the sound to smooth out sudden level changes."* and *"Compressors can add punch and definition to a track making it stand out from the rest of the mix."*
  - Delay: *"Delay effect repeats a sound like an echo."*
  - Reverb: *"Reverb simulates the natural reflections of a sound in a surrounding space."*
  - EQ: *"EQ (short for equalization) lets you change the level of selected frequencies in a sound."*
  - Distortion: *"Distortion and overdrive effects change the tone of the original sound, making it sound 'dirty' or 'fuzzy.'"*
  - Bitcrusher: *"The result is a lo-fi digital sound similar to old video games or drum machines."*
  - Tremolo: *"Tremolo raises and lowers the volume of a sound at regular intervals, creating a wobble effect."*
  - Phaser/Chorus (as "Modulation"): chorus, flanger, and phaser *"repeat the sound along with the original"* and shift it, *"often a shimmery or swirly effect on voices and instruments."*
  - Pitch shift (as "Vocal Transformer"): *"The Vocal Transformer changes the pitch and tone of your voice."*
- **[GarageBand Mac Smart Control types](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband/gbndf553bab8/mac)** — *"Effects controls often include Tremolo, which adds a wobbling effect to the sound, or Chorus, which thickens the sound."* Also: *"Ambience adjusts the size of the acoustic space"*, *"Reverb changes the reverb amount, or length of the reverb tail"*, *"Compression adds punch to the sound."*, *"Phaser adds a sweeping type of sound."*, *"Delay adds an echo effect."*
- **[GarageBand iPad Audio Recorder](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-ipad/record-sounds-with-the-audio-recorder-chs39283a21/ipados)** — the only literal consumer gate wording found: *"A noise gate cuts off the sound when it falls below a certain minimum level."* Also the autotune preset *"Extreme Tuning"*: *"a popular vocal effect that changes the pitch of your recording based on your song's key and scale settings."*
- **[Voice Memos](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/play-it-back-iphebbd067ce/ios)** — *"Enhance Recording"*: *"reduce background noise and echo when you play the recording"*; *"Skip Silence"* auto-skips gaps.
- **[Mic Modes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101993)** — Voice Isolation: *"Prioritizes your voice and blocks ambient noises."*

## Synthesis — one CANDIDATE plain label per registered node type

Plain half conventions used: verb-led or effect-described-plainly, 2–5 words, no jargon ("dynamics", "transient", "DSP", "attenuation" never appear in the candidates below), US English.

| Node type | Technical label | CANDIDATE plain label | Alternates | Support / disagreement note |

## Disagreements and edge cases

1. **"Echo" is the most overloaded word in the survey — and it points in opposite directions.** Karaoke gear *adds* echo: the ECHO knob is the category-standard control (Singing Machine: *"Rotate the ECHO control to add an echo effect to your voice"*), and BONAOK's spec admits the knob is a blend: *"Reverb Mode: Echo sound reverberation."* Apple maps delay→echo (*"Delay adds an echo effect"*), Voloco names the effect "Echo" and parenthesizes the technical name (*"Echo (Add Delay)"*). But podcast tools *remove* echo: Adobe *"Remove noise and echo"*, Descript *"reducing background noise, echo"*, Voice.ai literally ships an *"Echo Remover"*. Practical consequence: voxchain's plain labels should spend "echo" only on `delay`, use room-language for `reverb`, and never describe the gate as "removing echo."
2. **Gate is invisible as a gate; visible as "noise suppression."** No consumer speech product shows a gate. The user-facing feature is Zoom's *"Background noise suppression"*, Krisp's *"Remove background noises"*, Apple's Voice Isolation (*"Prioritizes your voice and blocks ambient noises"*). GarageBand is the only product with a literal, explained *"noise gate"* (*"cuts off the sound when it falls below a certain minimum level"*). Voloco's Clean is technically filtering but marketed purely as removing background noise. The CANDIDATE ("Cuts background noise") adopts the consumer framing while staying truthful to gate behavior (cutting, not canceling).
3. **Reverb is named by room size, never by mechanism.** Voloco presets (*Small Room, Large Room, Hall, Cathedral*), GarageBand's *"Ambience… size of the acoustic space"*, Smule's *"room size"* knob. "Space" (Voloco's *"Add Space"*) is the one-word alternative if the room metaphor ever feels too literal.
4. **Limiter is hidden everywhere.** Adobe, Descript, Riverside, Krisp, Voloco, Smule, StarMaker, KaraFun: none exposes a limiter. BandLab is the single exception (*"Clean Limiter… without crushing your dynamics"*). The candidate borrows that protective register ("caps", "keeps in check") rather than inventing engineering language.
5. **Autotune splits into two products in users' minds**: "help me sound like a pro" (BandLab *"stay on pitch"*, StarMaker *"sound like a pro singer"*, Voloco *"Natural Tune… pitch correction that's less obvious"*) vs "make me a robot/T-Pain" (Voloco *"Hard Tune — The classic, 'digital' pitch correction"*, Voicemod *"Voicetune for a musical effect"*, GarageBand *"Extreme Tuning"*). The CANDIDATE describes the default (natural) reading; the robotic reading belongs to preset names, not the node label.
6. **Tremolo and phaser are effectively invisible in voice products.** Only Apple documents them in plain words ("wobble", "sweeping"); BandLab's *"Gater"* proves the on/off-pulse sound exists in consumer apps but under a rhythmic-trick name, not as tremolo. Candidates follow Apple's verbs because there is no competing consumer vocabulary.
7. **Chorus vs doubling.** Apple says chorus *"thickens the sound"*; Voloco's Big Chorus and Smule's "doubling" blur the line (TC-Helicon tags them as separate facets). If voxchain ever adds a doubler, "Thickens your voice" cannot serve both.
8. **"Gain" never appears in consumer UIs — it's always "volume."** The plain half should say volume; the technical half keeps Gain. (Karaoke gear additionally distinguishes *microphone* volume from *music* volume — a useful future clarification in voxchain's mixer context.)
9. **Vibe-preset naming is the alternative everything-consumers pattern.** StarMaker (*"Distant, Warm, Vinyl, Party, Fascinating"*) and Smule (*Old Soul, Pro Studio, Polish*) name outcomes, not effects. This validates outcome-led phrasing ("Puts you in a room", "Old video-game sound") over mechanism-led phrasing.

## Scope note

- **De-esser**: listed in the ticket, but no de-esser node is registered in this codebase (the 14 `src/node-*.js` registrations are autotune, delay, gate, reverb, bitcrusher, distortion, compressor, limiter, pitchshift, tremolo, phaser, chorus, eq, gain — no de-esser), so it gets **no label** here. For whenever one is registered: the only consumer-visible de-esser name found is Voloco's free EQ preset *"De-Ess"* ([source](https://help.voloco.com/hc/en-us/articles/36347496022423-Complete-Voloco-Effects-Guide)), and Smule references *"sibilance algorithms"* ([source](https://blog.smule.com/sing-with-audio-fx-2024/)) without user-facing wording — i.e., de-essing is as invisible in consumer products as limiting.
- **Gain**: registered (`src/node-gain.js`) but unlisted in the ticket — included above with the other 13.
